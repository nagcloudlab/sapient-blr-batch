Lab 09 • The Problem2
Act 1 • The Anti-Pattern

The Watermelon SLA

Green on the outside. Red on the inside.

The report says

  • "98% of SLAs met!" — green checkmark
  • Response targets all hit
  • Resolution numbers look great

The reality

  • Users wait 45 min on hold (but SLA starts at ticket creation)
  • Incidents "resolved" by closing without fixing
  • P1s downgraded to P3 to dodge breach
  • Customer satisfaction: 2.1 / 5.0

How to avoid

  • Measure outcomes, not just outputs
  • Track re-open rates (was it truly fixed?)
  • Include CSAT surveys after resolution
  • Audit priority changes (P1 → P3 gaming)
  • Use XLAs (Experience Level Agreements) alongside SLAs
XLA example: SLA says "resolved in 45 min." XLA asks: "Were you satisfied? Was your work disrupted?"

Act 1 • The Key Concept

SLA vs SLO vs SLI

TermWhat It IsUPI Example
SLAFormal agreement between provider and customer. Contains multiple SLOs. Has contractual weight."NPCI UPI Platform Support Agreement" signed with member banks
SLOSpecific measurable target within the SLA. "What we promise.""99.95% availability per month" or "P1 resolved within 1 hour"
SLIThe actual metric that measures the SLO. "What we measure."Prometheus up metric, histogram_quantile(0.95,...)
The hierarchy:
SLA contains multiple SLOs. Each SLO is measured by one or more SLIs.

SLA → "We agree to these service levels" (Agreement)
SLO → "Specifically, we target 99.95% uptime" (Objective)
SLI → "We measure uptime using Prometheus up" (Indicator)

Act 2 • How It Works

ServiceNow SLA engine — two tables

SLA Definition contract_sla

The template. You create 7 of these. They sit waiting for matching tasks.

  • Which tasks does this apply to?
  • When does the clock start?
  • When does it pause? Stop?
  • What is the target duration?
  • What schedule applies?

Task SLA task_sla

The instance. Created automatically when a matching task appears.

  • Reference to the SLA Definition
  • Reference to the Task (INC0010001)
  • Start time, breach time
  • Current state (In Progress, Paused...)
  • Percentage elapsed
  • Has breached (true/false)
You never create Task SLAs manually. Create the SLA Definition. ServiceNow handles the rest. When Priority=1 AND State=New, the engine auto-attaches a 30-minute response timer.

Act 2 • SLA States

The SLA lifecycle

START CONDITION MET (e.g., Priority = 1 AND State = New)
    |
    |  ServiceNow creates a task_sla record.
    |  Breach time = now + duration (adjusted for schedule).
    v
CLOCK TICKING  ---------> PAUSE? (Awaiting User Info)
    |                          |
    |                    Clock stops. Breach time pushed forward.
    |                    Resume when pause condition clears.
    |                          |
    v                          v
STOP CONDITION MET? -----> YES --> SLA ACHIEVED (if before breach)
    |                              SLA BREACHED (if past due)
    | NO
    v
BREACH TIME REACHED -----> SLA BREACHED
    |                       (clock keeps ticking for reporting
    v                        "how far past breach")
STOP CONDITION EVENTUALLY MET --> Final state recorded.
Achieved: Stop condition met before breach time
Breached: Breach time reached before stop condition

Lab 09 • SLA Definitions8
Act 2 • Step 2

Create SLA schedules

Schedules determine when the clock ticks. A "4-hour SLA" with business hours means 4 hours of business time.

NPCI 24x7 Critical Support

For P1 and P2. Clock ticks all day, every day.

Mon-Sun: 00:00 - 23:59
Timezone: Asia/Kolkata

NPCI Business Hours

For P3 and P4. Mon-Sat 8 AM - 8 PM IST.

Mon-Sat: 08:00 - 20:00
Sunday:  excluded
Holidays: excluded (child schedule)
Timezone: Asia/Kolkata

Why this matters

Scenario: P3 incident created Friday 4:00 PM.
Schedule: Business Hours (Mon-Sat 8AM-8PM).

Clock ticks: 4PM-8PM Fri = 4 hours
Overnight: paused (8PM-8AM)
Clock ticks: 8AM-8PM Sat = 12 hours (need 20 more)

Without schedule: breach at 8 PM Friday
With schedule: breach pushes to next business week
Common pitfall: Setting timezone to UTC instead of Asia/Kolkata. Business hours become 1:30 PM - 1:30 AM IST. SLAs completely wrong.

How SLIs flow from infrastructure to incident to SLA clock:

    |  /actuator/prometheus

    |  SLI: up, http_server_requests, histogram_quantile

End-to-end automation: A Prometheus metric breaches an SLO → alert fires → incident created in ServiceNow → SLA timer starts → escalation notifications at 50%, 75%, 100%. No human intervention needed to start the clock.

Act 3 • Full Lifecycle

Walk through the complete SLA lifecycle

Step 1 Save P1 incident — Response SLA (30 min) starts. Stage: In Progress.
Step 2 Move to In Progress — Response SLA = Achieved. Resolution SLA (1 hr) starts.
Step 3 Set State = Awaiting User Info — Resolution SLA pauses. Breach time pushed forward.
Step 4 Move back to In Progress — Resolution SLA resumes. Clock ticking again.
Step 5 Resolve the incident — Resolution SLA = Achieved (if within 1 hour minus pause).
SLA Timeline with Pause:

NPCI UPI - P1 Critical Resolution (1 hour)
|=========         ========         ==================|

Final result: Both Task SLAs show Stage = Achieved, Has Breached = false. The incident was responded to within 30 minutes and resolved within 1 hour (excluding pause time).
