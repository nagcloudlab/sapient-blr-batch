Act 1 • Root Cause

What is "Tribal Knowledge"?

In simple terms: "Tribal knowledge" means information that exists only inside one person's head. It never gets written down. When that person is on leave, asleep, or leaves the company, the knowledge disappears with them. The "tribe" (team) depends on one person, and that is a fragile, dangerous setup.

Signs you have tribal knowledge

  • "Ask Ravi — he knows how to fix this"
  • "The runbook is in Ravi's head"
  • "We escalate everything to Ravi"
  • "When Ravi is on leave, we're stuck"
  • "New joiners shadow Ravi for 3 weeks"

What you want instead

  • The fix is written down and searchable
  • Any L1 agent can follow the runbook
  • New joiners read the KB on Day 1
  • Knowledge survives team changes
  • Ravi's expertise belongs to the team, not just Ravi

Act 1 • The Framework

What is the DIKW Model?

In simple terms: DIKW is a pyramid with four layers. Raw numbers at the bottom, wisdom at the top. Each layer adds more meaning and usefulness to what came before it.

Data

Raw numbers and logs. No context. Example: error_code=U01, count=847. Alone, this tells you nothing actionable.

Information

Data with context added. "847 U01 errors happened between 10:00-10:15 IST, memory at 94%." Now you know when and under what conditions.

Knowledge

Patterns you can act on. "U01 errors spike when memory exceeds 80%. Restarting the service clears the pool in 5 minutes." This is what a KB article captures.

Wisdom

Strategic understanding. "Pre-scale UPI 48 hours before festivals because volume spikes 300%." Prevents the problem from ever occurring.

Act 1 • The Methodology

What is KCS?

In simple terms: KCS stands for Knowledge-Centered Service. The core idea is brutally simple: write the article while you solve the problem, not later. "Later" never happens. When you're fixing an incident at 2 AM, that's exactly when you have the most knowledge about it. Capture it then.

The old way

  • Fix the incident
  • Tell yourself "I'll write it up later"
  • Later never arrives
  • Knowledge lives in Slack, email, memory
  • Next person starts from zero

The KCS way

  • Fix the incident
  • Click "Create Knowledge" right on the incident form
  • Article is drafted while context is fresh
  • SME approves it
  • Next person finds the answer in 30 seconds

How ServiceNow Implements KCS

Don't write docs after the fact. Capture knowledge in the workflow.

KCS Principles

  • Capture in the workflow: Resolving an incident? Document it now, not "later"
  • Evolve with demand: Articles improve each time they are used
  • Collective experience: Not one person's notes — the team's shared brain
  • Reward sharing: Recognize contributors, not hoarders

In ServiceNow

  • "Create Knowledge" button on Incident form
  • Article feedback and flagging
  • Usage analytics (views, helpfulness)
  • Suggested articles on incident creation
  • Knowledge workflow: Draft → Review → Publish
The lifecycle: Create (Draft) → Review (SME validates) → Publish (visible to team) → Archive → Retire. Every article is approved before going live.

Act 2 • Known Error Database

What is a "Known Error"?

In simple terms: A Known Error is a problem where you know what is wrong and you have a workaround, but you have not yet permanently fixed the root cause. Think of it like a leaky tap: you know the washer is worn out (root cause identified), you've put a bucket under it (workaround documented), but you haven't called the plumber yet (permanent fix pending).

ITIL flow

  • Incident: "UPI is down!"
  • Problem: "Why does this keep happening?" (root cause analysis)
  • Known Error: "Root cause found, workaround documented, permanent fix in progress"
  • Change: Permanent fix deployed
  • Known Error retired: Problem solved for good

Why track Known Errors?

  • L1 agents find the workaround immediately when the same incident recurs
  • No wasted time re-investigating solved problems
  • Permanent fix is tracked to completion
  • KEDB = Known Error DataBase

Act 2 • Approval Workflow

Why Can't Articles Go Live Immediately?

In simple terms: If anyone could publish anything to the KB with zero review, imagine the damage. An agent writes a runbook at 3 AM under pressure and gets one step wrong. Now every L1 agent follows that wrong step. A wrong article can be worse than no article. The approval workflow is your quality gate.

Without approval

  • Incorrect steps published immediately
  • Every agent follows wrong procedure
  • Incidents made worse, not better
  • No accountability for accuracy

With approval workflow

  • Author writes the article (Draft)
  • SME validates accuracy (Review)
  • Only then does it go live (Published)
  • Team trusts what they read in the KB
