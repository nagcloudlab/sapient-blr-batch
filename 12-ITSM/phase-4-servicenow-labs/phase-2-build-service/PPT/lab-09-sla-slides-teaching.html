Why the watermelon fools everyone

What happensIn simple terms

Lab 09 • Watermelon Explained5
Act 1 • The Fix

How to avoid the watermelon

  • Measure outcomes — Did the user's problem actually get solved? Not just "was the ticket closed?"
  • Track re-open rates — If tickets keep getting re-opened, they weren't truly fixed
  • CSAT surveys — Ask the customer "Are you happy?" after resolution. Their answer is the real truth.
  • Audit priority changes — Flag any P1 → P3 changes and investigate. Catch gaming.
  • Use XLAs — Experience Level Agreements measure the user's experience, not just the clock
SLA vs XLA:
SLA asks: "Was the ticket closed in 45 minutes?" — yes/no, just a timer
XLA asks: "Was the customer satisfied? Was their work disrupted?" — measures real human experience
Lab 09 • Avoiding Watermelon6
Act 1 • Key Term

CSAT — Customer Satisfaction Score

  • A survey sent to the user after an incident is resolved
  • Typically a simple rating: 1-5 stars or "How satisfied were you?"
  • Measures whether the customer actually felt helped
  • Not about whether the ticket was closed on time — about the real experience
Example:
SLA report says: "Resolved in 30 minutes" — looks great
CSAT survey says: "1 star — my issue wasn't actually fixed" — reveals the truth

CSAT is your lie detector for watermelon SLAs.

Act 1 • The Key Concept

SLA vs SLO vs SLI

TermWhat It IsSimple Analogy
SLAFormal agreement/contract between provider and customer. Contains multiple SLOs.The full contract you sign
SLOSpecific measurable target within the SLA. "What we promise."A specific promise in the contract
SLIThe actual metric/number that measures the SLO. "What we measure."The thermometer that checks the promise
The hierarchy:

SLA → "We agree to these service levels" (the Agreement)
SLO → "Specifically, we target 99.95% uptime" (the Objective)
SLI → "We measure uptime using Prometheus up metric" (the Indicator)

Act 2 • How It Works

ServiceNow SLA engine — two tables

The entire SLA system runs on just two tables:

SLA Definition contract_sla

The template/rule. You create it once.

  • Which tasks does this apply to?
  • When does the clock start?
  • When does it pause? Stop?
  • What is the target duration?
  • What schedule applies?

Task SLA task_sla

The live timer. Created automatically.

  • Which SLA Definition it came from
  • Which incident it's attached to
  • Start time and breach time
  • Current state (In Progress, Paused...)
  • Percentage elapsed
  • Has breached (true/false)

Act 2 • SLA States

The SLA lifecycle

START CONDITION MET (e.g., Priority = 1 AND State = New)
    |
    |  ServiceNow creates a task_sla record.
    |  Breach time = now + duration (adjusted for schedule).
    v
CLOCK TICKING  ---------> PAUSE? (Awaiting User Info)
    |                          |
    |                    Clock stops. Breach time pushed forward.
    |                    Resume when pause condition clears.
    |                          |
    v                          v
STOP CONDITION MET? -----> YES --> SLA ACHIEVED (if before breach)
    |                              SLA BREACHED (if past due)
    | NO
    v
BREACH TIME REACHED -----> SLA BREACHED
    |                       (clock keeps ticking for reporting)
    v
STOP CONDITION EVENTUALLY MET --> Final state recorded.
Achieved: Finished before the deadline
Breached: Deadline passed before finishing

Act 2 • Step 2

Create SLA schedules

Schedules determine when the clock ticks.

NPCI 24x7 Critical Support

For P1 and P2. Clock ticks all day, every day.

Mon-Sun: 00:00 - 23:59
Timezone: Asia/Kolkata

NPCI Business Hours

For P3 and P4. Mon-Sat 8 AM - 8 PM IST.

Mon-Sat: 08:00 - 20:00
Sunday:  excluded
Holidays: excluded
Timezone: Asia/Kolkata

Why this matters

Example: P3 incident created Friday 4:00 PM.

Clock ticks: 4PM-8PM Fri = 4 hours
Overnight: paused (8PM-8AM)
Saturday: clock ticks again

Without schedule: breach at 8 PM Friday
With schedule: breach pushes to next business week
Lab 09 • Schedules16
Act 2 • Common Mistake

Schedule pitfall: wrong timezone

Setting timezone to UTC instead of Asia/Kolkata

Your business hours (8 AM - 8 PM) become 1:30 PM - 1:30 AM IST.

SLAs become completely wrong. Morning incidents won't have ticking clocks until afternoon.
Always set: Timezone = Asia/Kolkata for Indian operations.
Double-check this on every schedule you create.

Walk through the complete SLA lifecycle

Step 1 Save P1 incident — Response SLA (30 min) starts
Step 2 Move to In Progress — Response SLA = Achieved. Resolution SLA (1 hr) starts.

Step 5 Resolve incident — Resolution SLA = Achieved (if within 1 hour minus pause).
