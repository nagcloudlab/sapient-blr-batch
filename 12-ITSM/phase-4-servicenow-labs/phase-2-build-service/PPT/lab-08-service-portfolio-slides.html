Act 1 • The Problem

The VP asks three simple questions

Mon 9:00 VP walks into Sanjay's office: "What services does UPI run?"
Mon 9:01 Sanjay: "Well, there's the payment platform, and settlement, and..." He opens a spreadsheet.
Mon 9:03 VP: "Which service is most critical? What does each cost? Who owns them?"
Mon 9:05 Sanjay checks Confluence, an old wiki, and a PowerPoint from Q1. Different answers everywhere.
Mon 9:10 VP: "We have 12 CIs in the CMDB. But which BUSINESS SERVICE do they support? We can't manage what we can't define."
The CMDB tells you WHAT exists. The Service Portfolio tells you WHY it exists and WHO it serves.

With Portfolio

Business vs Technical vs Application Service

ServiceNow models services in a class hierarchy, just like CI classes.

Business Servicecmdb_ci_service_businessCustomer-facing, delivers business valueUPI Payment Processing

2. Information & Technology: PostgreSQL, Spring Boot, Prometheus
3. Partners & Suppliers: Member Banks (SBI, HDFC), RBI Compliance

Act 2 • The Payoff

Impact analysis: PostgreSQL goes down

With the dependency chain built, ServiceNow can trace impact automatically:

PostgreSQL Primary (CI) goes down
    |
    v  (Used by)
UPI Database Service (Technical Service)
    |
    v  (Used by)
UPI Payment Processing (Business Service)   -- ALL UPI payments affected
UPI Dispute Resolution (Business Service)   -- Disputes cannot be processed
UPI Merchant Onboarding (Business Service)  -- Onboarding halted
Without Portfolio: "The database is down." Nobody knows the business impact. Takes 30 minutes to figure out who's affected.
With Portfolio: "UPI Payment Processing, Dispute Resolution, and Merchant Onboarding are all impacted." Instant. Automated. Accurate.

Act 3 • Prove It Works

Scenario challenge: Retire a service

Management decides to retire "UPI Dispute Resolution." Sanjay opens ServiceNow and changes Service Status to Retired.

Using your Service Portfolio, answer:

#QuestionAnswer
1Does it disappear from Business Services list?Depends on your list filter (Status = Catalog won't show it)
2Can you still see it in All Services?Yes — retired services remain in the Portfolio
3Are dependent Technical Services also retired?NO. Transaction Processing and Database Service are shared.
4What must you do before retiring?Impact analysis: check all "Used by" relationships first.
Retiring a Business Service does NOT cascade. Technical Services may be shared by multiple Business Services. This is why dependency mapping matters.
