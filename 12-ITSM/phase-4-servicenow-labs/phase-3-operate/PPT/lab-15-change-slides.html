Act 1 • Decision Framework

The 7 Rs of Change Management

Before any change is approved, the CAB must answer all 7 questions.

#QuestionOur PostgreSQL Separation Change
R1RAISED -- Who raised the change?Ravi Kumar, from Problem PRB (Lab 14)
R2REASON -- Why is the change needed?SPOF: both DBs on same server = no failover
R3RETURN -- What's the expected benefit?Eliminate SPOF, enable real DB failover
R4RISKS -- What are the risks?Data migration errors, replication lag, downtime
R5RESOURCES -- What resources are needed?New server, DBA time, 3-hour maintenance window
R6RESPONSIBLE -- Who is accountable?Ravi Kumar (implement), Sanjay (approve)
R7RELATIONSHIP -- What other changes depend on this?Circuit breaker (Lab 14), settlement optimization
ITIL 4: The 7 Rs ensure every change is thoroughly evaluated before authorization. Skipping any R is how outages happen.

Act 3 • When Things Go Wrong

Failed Change: Rollback Scenario

Not every change succeeds. That's why the rollback plan is mandatory.

Scenario: PostgreSQL 14 to 16 Upgrade

02:00 Normal change begins. Full backup taken.
02:30 pg_upgrade running...
02:45 Migration script FAILS at step 3. Foreign key constraint violation on settlement_transactions.
02:50 Decision: ROLLBACK.
03:05 PostgreSQL 14 restored from backup. All settlements processing normally.

Close as Unsuccessful

FieldValue
StateClosed
Close codeUnsuccessful
Close notesMigration script FK violation. Rollback completed. Remediation: fix FK constraints, retest, reschedule.
A failed change is not a failure of process. It proves the process works -- the rollback plan saved us from a real outage.

ConceptDefinition
