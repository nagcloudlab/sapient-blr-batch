Trainer guide

Each deck opens with the story from its Markdown source, then teaches the concepts through named examples and records. A chapter handoff connects it to the next topic before the final discussion question. Reveal the suggested answer after discussion. Use the Notes button for slide-specific guidance and the complete original Markdown source. Notes are visible on the current screen: keep that in mind while screen sharing.

Presenting and exporting

Open index.html in a browser; no server or internet connection is required. Arrow keys, Space, Page Up/Down, Home and End navigate. N opens notes; O opens overview; F toggles fullscreen. B hides the slide for discussion; press B or Escape to restore it. Interactive slides let learners classify support cases and simulate a shared-database failure. The final slide links directly to the next lesson. Print / PDF prints one 16:9 slide per page, including discussion answers. In the print dialog choose Save as PDF, enable background graphics and disable browser headers and footers. A projector or landscape display gives the best presentation view.

Editorial treatment

The eleven original Markdown files are unchanged. Slides condense the notes into teachable concepts, retaining their connected payment scenario. NPCI and bank names in the original notes are examples, not evidence of actual events, systems or agreements. Unsupported current transaction counts, user counts, regulatory consequences and the “80% of incidents” statistic have been omitted.

  • Availability calculations use a declared 30-day month. Error budgets use an explicit eligible-request count.
  • An SLO commonly offers margin above an SLA; it is not universally required to be stricter.
  • Change authorization follows a designated authority and policy. CAB is not a universal approval gate. Recovery can require alternatives to rollback.
  • A problem is a cause or potential cause of incidents; a known error is an analyzed but unresolved problem.
  • The CMDB impact exercise explicitly assumes a shared database, resolving an inconsistency between the original diagram and explanation.
  • The knowledge deck teaches runbook structure rather than reproducing unvalidated commands: the source mixes an Oracle CI with psql, and calls an application after scaling it to zero.
  • Priority matrices, fulfillment targets, escalation times and roles are illustrative. The PDCA results are example observations, not production evidence.

Illustrations and editing

Eleven original illustrations were created using the built-in image-generation tool, guided by your watercolor reference. Base teaching content lives in build/content.py; build/enhancements.py selects teaching visuals; build/story.py restores the audience-visible narrative, exact records and chapter connections; run python3 slides/build/build.py from the source folder to regenerate HTML.
