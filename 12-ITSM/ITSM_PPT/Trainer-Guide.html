How the animation works

  1. The illustration and character dialogue appear when you open a slide.
  2. Press Right or Space to reveal the term, explanation, points or one table row at a time. On the 16 concept animations, the diagram changes and a teaching point appears at each step.
  3. After the final reveal, the next press advances to the next slide. Left steps backward.
  4. Press R or choose Replay slide to restart the current explanation. Press A for all content on the current slide. Shift + Right skips to the next slide.
  5. Play story or S automatically advances one step at your chosen pace: 5, 8 or 12 seconds. Click again to pause. Playback stops at discussion questions. The animation is silent.

F enters full screen. O opens the slide list. G opens searchable chapter terms. Home and End jump to the beginning and end. Horizontal swipes also work.

Select Tools, then Controls and help to disable motion. Browser reduced-motion preferences are also respected.

Keeping trainer notes separate

Press P or select Tools → Trainer window to open a separate notes window with reveal controls. Share only the slide window with learners. Allow the popup if your browser blocks it. N opens notes inside the slide window, where learners can see them.

  5. At a discussion slide, pause before revealing the suggested response.

How the notes became the story

The 11 Markdown files retain their chapter order and concepts. The opening chaos becomes Priya’s coordination story. The incident, problem and change chapters follow the same Bank A issue. Arjun then needs access and capacity, discovers the catalog and reviews supporting configurations and commitments. Priya’s night shift introduces knowledge. The final chapter measures an improvement and returns to Meera and Uma.

The NPCI / UPI setting provides the domain context. The people, Bank A incident, internal applications, targets and policies are fictional. They do not describe actual NPCI procedures.

Clarifications retained for accurate teaching

  • Apps, TPAPs and PSP banks have distinct roles. Merchant onboarding and app participation are separate activities.
  • A service does not remove every consumer cost or risk. An incident’s restoration can happen before its underlying problem is resolved.
  • Problems can have known causes. Known errors are analyzed problems that remain unresolved.
  • Change authority follows risk and governance. A CAB meeting is not mandatory for every normal change. Emergency work uses appropriate expedited authorization.
  • Requests still require applicable identity, entitlement and approval checks. A capacity increase is not automatically a standard change.
  • The CMDB records selected configurations and relationships. The shared-database example consistently uses Database T and Cluster D.
  • SLI, SLO and SLA use a defined scope and window. An SLO is not always stricter than an SLA by definition. Remedies depend on the agreement.
  • Availability uses an exact 30-day example. Request-based error budgets keep one denominator. The final improvement calculation reaches 99.4976%, still below its 99.9% SLO.
  • The runbook teaches context, decisions, verification and escalation. The source’s unvalidated production commands are not executable guidance in this course.
  • Unverified scale, outage, regulatory and percentage claims are replaced with clearly identified teaching examples.

02 · The improvement cycle repeats

PDCA means Plan, Do, Check, Act and is also called the Deming cycle. It repeats rather than ending after rollout.

  1. Plan: Choose an opportunity. Define the baseline, target and measures.
  2. Do: Try a controlled improvement at an appropriate scale.

  4. Act: Adopt, adapt or stop. Carry what you learned into the next plan.
  5. The next cycle: Return to planning with the new baseline and the remaining gap.
